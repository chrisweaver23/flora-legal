Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 14, 2026

Flora ("the app") is a gut-health food tracker. This policy explains what data Flora handles and where it goes. The short version: your data lives on your phone. A Flora account is optional, and — unless you turn on the optional, off-by-default cloud backup described below — no Flora server stores your meals, scores, or profile.

What Flora stores, and where

Everything you enter in Flora — your profile (age, height, weight, sex, activity level, goals, allergies), your logged meals, scores, sensitivity reports, learned triggers, saved recipes, grocery list, and water tracking — is stored locally on your device. Deleting the app deletes this data. "Reset app & onboarding" inside the app also erases it.

A Flora account is optional: you can create one with Sign in with Apple (described below), and everything in Flora works without it. There is no automatic cloud sync — the optional, off-by-default cloud backup (described below) is the one exception, and you control it. There are no third-party analytics or advertising SDKs. The App Store version of Flora sends a small stream of anonymous usage signals to Flora's own server (described below) — never to an analytics company.

What leaves your device

Some features send limited data to third-party services to work:

  • AI analysis (Anthropic Claude). When you use photo scanning, spoken/typed meal analysis, Ask Flora, or AI-generated recipes, the relevant content is sent to Anthropic's API. For Ask Flora that context is: your question, your profile (first name, age, sex, height, weight, activity level, allergies and your daily calorie and macro targets), your two gut scores, your goals and learned triggers, your recent meals and their scores, your latest gut check-in, and your current grocery list — this is what makes the answer about you rather than generic. For photo or typed meal analysis it is the photo or description itself through Flora's proxy server to generate the response. Flora's proxy does not store this content; Anthropic processes it under its own API data policies.
  • Barcode and product lookups (OpenFoodFacts). Scanned barcodes and product-name searches are sent to the free OpenFoodFacts database to fetch label data and product images. No personal data is included.
  • Nearby restaurants (OpenStreetMap Overpass). When you grant location permission, your approximate coordinates are sent to OpenStreetMap's public Overpass API to find restaurants near you. Location is used only in the moment, never stored off your device, and you can use the app fully without granting it.
  • Photography (Pexels). Recipe and article images are fetched from the Pexels photo API using food keywords only — no personal data.
  • Brand logos (Clearbit). Restaurant logos are fetched by restaurant domain — no personal data.
  • Anonymous usage signals (Flora's own server). So we can tell whether onboarding works and catch crashes, the App Store version of Flora sends Flora's own server small anonymous events: which onboarding step was viewed, a coarse band of the starting score shown at the reveal (like "60s" — never the exact number), that a purchase started, succeeded, was cancelled or failed (a coarse failure category only — never the store's error text), and an error's type and code location. Each event carries a random install id minted on your device — it is not your name, email, or a device identifier, and "Reset app & onboarding" mints a new one. The list above is illustrative rather than complete — Flora emits roughly forty such events covering navigation, feature use and milestones, all of the same shape. None of them ever include your health answers, symptoms, allergies, meals, exact scores, or profile, and they go only to Flora's own server — no third-party analytics service ever receives them. When you correct a scan — removing or adding an ingredient, picking an alternate identification, or reporting an ingredient's score as off — the corrected food names (never your health answers, symptoms or scores) are included in these anonymous signals so Flora's food database learns from real fixes. If you answer the one-time "Where'd you find Flora?" question after setup, your one-tap answer — and, if you pick "Somewhere else", the short note you can optionally type — is included the same way, so we know which channels to thank; skipping the question sends nothing.
  • Optional push notifications (anonymous push token, Flora's own server). Notifications are optional. If you enable them, the App Store version of Flora registers an anonymous push token — a delivery address for this device generated by the notification service, not your identity — with Flora's own server, stored under the same random install id as the usage signals above. It exists only so Flora can deliver its reminders and the occasional app update to your lock screen; it is never sold or shared, and delivery goes through Expo's push service (which routes it to Apple's push system). The token is deleted from Flora's server when you turn notifications off — in Flora's Notifications settings or in iOS Settings — or when you reset the app. With notifications off, no token is ever registered.
  • Optional weekly email report (Flora's own server) — only if you sign up. If you enter your email address for the weekly gut report (on a weekly report or in You → Privacy & data), Flora's own server stores that address together with the app's random install id — used only to send you the reports you asked for. It is never sold or shared, never used for advertising, and never linked to your meals, scores, or health answers (the emails themselves are written summaries, not your data). Unsubscribing — the in-app toggle, or replying "unsubscribe" to any report — deletes the address from Flora's server, and "Reset app & onboarding" deletes it too. If you never sign up, Flora never has your email.
  • Optional friend codes (Flora's own server). Flora's share message includes your personal friend code (like "FL-X7K2M9"), derived on your device from the app's random install id — the code is a one-way scramble that contains nothing about you, and sharing it sends nothing to any server. If a friend gives you their code and you enter it on Flora's paywall, Flora's own server stores that code together with this device's random install id, used only to count how many friends joined from each code so the sharer can be thanked. It contains no health data and no identity, is never sold or shared, and one device can count at most one code. The row is deleted from Flora's server when you use "Reset app & onboarding". If the in-app "Friends joined" counter is enabled, Flora asks its server how many devices entered your code — that request carries only the code itself.
  • Subscription processing (RevenueCat). Purchases go through Apple; Flora uses RevenueCat, a subscription-management service, to validate them. RevenueCat receives your App Store purchase receipts and the app's random install id — or, if you created a Flora account, the account's anonymous id (never your name, email, meals, or health answers), which lets Flora keep your subscription working across reinstalls and, if you shared a friend code, recognise when a friend you invited becomes a subscriber. RevenueCat processes this under its own privacy policy; "Reset app & onboarding" mints a new install id. To credit referral rewards honestly, RevenueCat also notifies Flora's server when a subscription is first paid or refunded; the server stores only the install id, the date of the first payment, and a refunded yes/no — never an amount, a product, or anything about you — and a refund removes the credit.
  • Optional Flora account (Sign in with Apple, Flora's own server) — only if you create one. Flora offers an account at the end of setup ("Not now" skips it) and in You → Account. Apple performs the sign in; Flora's own server verifies Apple's token and stores the stable, anonymous user identifier Apple assigns to Flora for you, your name and email address only if you choose to share them at that first sign in (an Apple "Hide My Email" relay address counts as your email), when you signed in, and the app's random install ids you have signed in from. The account exists for two things: your cloud backup (below) is kept under the account instead of the anonymous identifier, so a new phone restores it with one sign in, and your subscription is tied to the account through RevenueCat (below) so it follows you too. It is never sold or shared and never used for advertising. Sign out any time (your data stays on the phone; cloud backup pauses). Delete it any time in the app (You → Account → Delete account): Flora's server erases the account, its sign-in sessions and its cloud backup and revokes the Sign in with Apple authorization, then the app erases everything on the phone.
  • Optional cloud backup (Flora's own server) — off by default. If you turn on Cloud backup (You → Account → Privacy & data), Flora keeps one copy of your Flora data — your profile (including the first name you entered at setup, plus age, height, weight, sex, activity, goals and allergies), logged meals and scores, sensitivity reports and learned triggers, safe foods, check-ins, grocery list and saved recipes, as a single JSON file — on Flora's own server, refreshed about once a day, so a new phone can pick up where you left off. It is sent over an encrypted connection (TLS) and stored under an anonymous identifier (your subscription's anonymous billing id, or the app's random install id) — the identifier itself is never your name or email, though the backed-up profile inside the file does contain the first name you entered at setup. The copy is used only to restore your data to a phone you sign into with the same subscription or backup, is never sold or shared, and never trains any AI model. If you create a Flora account (Sign in with Apple, optional), the backup is kept under your account id instead of the anonymous identifier, and Flora stores the anonymous Apple user id plus your name and email if you chose to share them; deleting the account in the app (You → Account → Delete account) erases the account, the backup and revokes the Apple credential. You can delete the server copy at any time in the app: turn the toggle off and choose delete, or use "Reset app & onboarding" — both remove it. With the toggle off (the default), nothing is ever uploaded.

Permissions

  • Camera — photographing meals and scanning barcodes. Optional.
  • Photo library — choosing an existing meal photo. Optional.
  • Microphone — the optional "Speak it" meal logging listens while you describe your meal. Optional.
  • Speech recognition — what you say is transcribed on your device into text you review and edit before anything is scored; nothing you say is sent to a Flora server. If on-device recognition isn't available, the feature falls back to the keyboard's own dictation. Optional.
  • Location (while using) — finding restaurants near you. Optional.
  • Notifications — optional reminders (morning gut update, streak guard, post-meal check-in, weekly report, daily log reminder, trial reminders, away check-backs), scheduled on your device — never more than two a day, and never at night — plus the occasional update Flora sends through the push channel described above. Turning notifications off stops both and deletes the push token.

Every permission is optional; the relevant feature simply stays off without it.

Health disclaimer

Flora offers general wellness information, not medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Gut scores are informational estimates. Consult a qualified professional about medical concerns.

Children

Flora is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.
